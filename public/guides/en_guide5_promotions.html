Dumb promotion vs. smart promotion

A dumb promotion is giving a discount without knowing whether there is still profit left after. It is the kind that makes you work twice as hard to earn half as much. Many businesses go under on their best sales days, when they sell a lot but at a loss.

A smart promotion has a clear objective: move stale inventory, attract a new customer, raise the average order value, or build loyalty. And it always protects your minimum profit margin.

Before any discount, calculate this: After applying the discount, am I still making money? If you cannot answer that immediately, you should not be running the promotion.
Minimum discounted price = Total cost ÷ (1 – desired minimum profit %)
Example: If your product costs $40 all-in and you want at least 10% profit, the minimum price is $40 ÷ 0.90 = $44.44. Below that, you are working for free or at a loss.

7 promotion types that actually work

Type 1

Buy X, Get Y

The customer buys one product and gets another as a gift or at a discount. Ideal for moving complementary slow-moving stock and increasing order value.

Example: "Buy 2 t-shirts and get a pair of socks free." You sell 2 items at once. The socks clear from your slow-moving stock. Everyone wins.
Type 2

Flash Sale

A discount valid for a limited time (2 hours, 24 hours, until midnight). Urgency makes customers decide faster. Works great on Instagram Stories and messaging apps.

Example: "Today only until 8pm: 15% off all bags. Back to regular price after." Announce in the morning and send a reminder at 7pm.
Type 3

Volume Discount

The more the customer buys, the cheaper per unit. It increases the sale volume and lets you spread your fixed costs across more units.

Example: "1 soap: $15 | Kit of 3: $39 | Kit of 5: $60." Whoever buys 5 saves $15, and you sold 5x more in one transaction.

Type 4

VIP List Exclusive

Offer a discount or early access to customers on your VIP list. Builds loyalty with existing buyers and gives new ones a reason to sign up.

Example: "VIP members get access to the new product 48 hours before the public, plus a 10% exclusive discount."
Type 5

Clear Old Stock

Any product sitting unsold for more than 60 days is costing you space and cash. Better to sell it at a thinner margin than to keep it stagnant.

Example: "Special selection: up to 30% off. Limited stock, first come first served." Apply the discount only to those specific items, not the whole store.
Type 6

Holiday or Seasonal Promotion

Mother's Day, Christmas, Black Friday, your store's anniversary. Dates give the discount context so it feels special rather than desperate.

Example: "Mother's Day Week: free shipping on everything." Free shipping can cost less than a percentage discount and customers perceive it as a big benefit.
Type 7

Upfront Payment Discount

Offer a discount for immediate payment. You receive the money instantly with no processing fees, and you still move product faster.

Example: "Pay now: $89 | 3 installments: $94.50." The upfront discount offsets the card fee you are no longer paying, so your actual profit stays the same or improves.

Checklist before launching a promotion

  • Did I calculate the minimum price that still gives me a profit?
  • Does the promotion have a defined start and end date?
  • Do I know how many units I will make available?
  • Do I have a clear reason for the promotion (date, inventory, launch)?
  • Do I have a plan for how to promote it (messages, Stories, posts)?
  • Will I measure how many sales the promotion actually generated?
Final takeaway: A good promotion is not the maximum discount possible. It is the smallest discount that generates the result you need. Test it, measure it, adjust it. Whoever knows their numbers runs the game.
