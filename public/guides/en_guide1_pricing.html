The mistake that kills most businesses

Most people price their products like this: look at what competitors charge, go a bit lower, and hope money is left over. That is guesswork pricing and it destroys businesses.

Charging too much pushes customers away. Charging too little means you work for nothing. The right price covers every cost you have, adds your fair profit, and still leaves room for surprises.

Golden rule: Price is not what the market charges. Price is what makes you profitable after paying for everything.
Step 1 Cost of Goods

Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

COGS is everything you spent to have that product ready to sell: raw materials, inbound shipping, packaging, and losses.

  • What you paid for the product or raw materials
  • Inbound shipping: what you paid to receive your inventory
  • Packaging: bags, boxes, labels, tape
  • Losses and damage: typically 2% to 5% of stock

Step 2 Fixed Costs Per Unit

Spreading your fixed costs

Fixed costs are what you pay every month regardless of sales: rent, electricity, phone, internet, software. You need to divide those costs across every product you sell that month.

Fixed cost per product = Total monthly fixed costs ÷ Units sold per month

Step 4 Final Price

The correct pricing formula

With all costs in hand, apply this formula. It ensures the final price already absorbs platform fees, taxes, and still delivers the profit margin you defined.

Price = (COGS + Fixed cost per unit) ÷ (1 − % Variable costs − % Desired profit)
Full example: Handmade bag
ItemValue
Product cost (COGS)$22.00
Fixed cost per unit$10.00
Platform fee (15%)included in formula
Tax (6%)included in formula
Desired profit (20%)included in formula

Calculation: (22 + 10) ÷ (1 - 0.15 - 0.06 - 0.20) = $32 ÷ 0.59 = $54.23

Charging $55 covers everything and still delivers 20% real profit on the sale price.

The 3x shortcut

Many retail segments use 3x COGS as a quick starting point for validation.

COGS $22.00 x 3 = $66.00
Useful for a sanity check, not a substitute for the full calculation.

Checklist before setting your price

Before publishing any product, go through each item below. If anything is missing, your price may be wrong without you realizing it.

  • Did I calculate the cost of each product individually?
  • Did I include inbound shipping and packaging in COGS?
  • Did I estimate losses and damage from my inventory?
  • Did I divide my fixed costs by my monthly sales volume?
  • Did I include platform fees or card processing fees?
  • Did I account for applicable taxes?
  • Did I define a profit percentage before calculating the price?
  • Did I check whether the final price is competitive in my market?
