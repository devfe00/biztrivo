The mistake that kills most businesses

Most people price their products like this: they look at what competitors charge, go a bit lower, and hope there is money left over. That is guesswork pricing and it destroys businesses.

Charging too much pushes customers away. Charging too little means you work for nothing. The right price covers every cost you have, adds your fair profit, and still leaves room for surprises.

Golden rule: Price is not what the market charges. Price is what makes you profitable after paying for everything.

Step 1 — Cost of Goods (COGS)

COGS is everything you spent to have that product ready to sell.

  • How much you paid for the product or raw materials
  • Inbound shipping (what you paid to receive it)
  • Packaging (bags, boxes, labels, tape)
  • Losses and damage (usually 2% to 5% of stock)
Practical example

You sell handmade bags. Each bag uses $18 in materials, $3 in packaging, and you factor in $1 average loss. Product cost = $22.

Step 2 — Fixed Costs Per Unit

Fixed costs are what you pay every month regardless of sales: rent, electricity, phone, internet, software. You need to spread these costs across every product you sell.

Fixed cost per product = Total monthly fixed costs ÷ Units sold per month
Practical example

You have $800/month in fixed costs and sell 80 bags per month. Fixed cost per bag = $800 ÷ 80 = $10.

Step 3 — Variable Costs

These costs only exist when you make a sale. Most people forget them and then wonder where the profit went.

  • Marketplace or card fees (Amazon, Etsy, Shopify payments): usually 12% to 20%
  • Outbound shipping (when the customer does not pay it)
  • Sales commissions if you have a team
  • Taxes on revenue: varies by country and business type
Watch out: Many sellers list on marketplaces and forget the platform keeps up to 16% of every sale. Add shipping. Add taxes. Price with all of that already included.

Step 4 — The Final Price Formula

With all costs in hand, apply this formula:

Price = (COGS + Fixed cost per unit) ÷ (1 – % Variable costs – % Desired profit)

Checklist before setting your price

  • Did I calculate the cost of each product individually?
  • Did I include inbound shipping and packaging?
  • Did I divide my fixed costs by monthly sales volume?
  • Did I include platform or card processing fees?
  • Did I include applicable taxes?
  • Did I define a profit percentage before calculating?
  • Did I check whether the final price is competitive?
