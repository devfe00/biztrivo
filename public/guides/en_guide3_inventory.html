Why track inventory at all?

There are two scenarios that break businesses: selling something you do not have (leads to refunds, complaints, and angry customers) and buying more than you sell (cash sitting in unsold product).

Inventory control prevents both. With it you know exactly what you have, what is running low, what sells most, and when to restock.

Golden rule: Inventory is money sitting still. The more accurate your control, the less cash you lock up and the more capital you have to keep the business moving.

The 5 columns every spreadsheet needs

It does not need to be complicated. A Google Sheets or even a phone spreadsheet app works fine. What matters is tracking these 5 pieces of information:

Recording stock movements

Alongside the main sheet, keep a movements tab. Every time stock comes in or goes out, you log it.

Common mistake: Only logging sales and forgetting to record damage, gifts, and exchanges. Then the stock on paper does not match the real shelf. Record everything that leaves, whether it is a sale or a discard.

How to define your Minimum Stock level

Minimum stock is your reorder alert. When you hit that number, it is time to buy more. To calculate it, you need two pieces of information:

Minimum Stock = Average daily sales × Replenishment lead time (in days)
Practical example

You sell an average of 2 white shirts per day. Your supplier takes 5 days to deliver. So:

Minimum stock = 2 × 5 = 10 units

When stock drops to 10 pieces, place a new order immediately. You will never hit zero.

Step by step: set it up now

1

List all your products

Open a new spreadsheet. Put each product on its own row. If you have variations like color or size, each variation gets its own row.

2

Do a physical count

Physically count everything in stock today. This is your opening balance. Do not trust your memory. Count for real.

3

Set the minimum stock for each product

Use the formula above. For slow-moving items it might be 1 or 2 units. For top sellers, calculate carefully.

4

Log every movement from this point on

Every sale, every purchase, every damaged item. No exceptions. It takes 30 seconds per entry and makes all the difference.

5

Check physical stock against the spreadsheet once a week

If there is a difference, investigate. Small gaps are normal. Large ones signal a problem that needs attention.

Inventory checklist

  • Did I list all products with names and unique codes?
  • Did I do the initial physical count?
  • Did I set a minimum stock level for each item?
  • Do I have a movements tab for ins and outs?
  • Am I recording damage, gifts, and exchanges as outgoing stock?
  • Am I doing a weekly physical vs spreadsheet check?
Bonus tip: Use Google Sheets on your phone. Log outgoing stock at the moment of each sale, not later. Logging later is where errors creep in.
