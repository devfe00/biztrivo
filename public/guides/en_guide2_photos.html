Why photos matter so much

In a physical store, the customer picks up the product, feels it, tests it. Online, they only have the photo. The photo is the product. If it looks bad, dark, or careless, the customer assumes the product is exactly the same way.

The good news: you do not need a professional camera. Today's phones shoot better than studio cameras from ten years ago. What is missing is technique, and you are learning it right now.

Golden rule: Good photo = good light + clean background + organized product. That is it. There is no bigger secret than that.

The one thing that changes everything: Light

Light is ingredient number one. Without it, no camera in the world can save the photo.

Use natural light whenever possible

Place your product near a window with daylight, but without direct sunlight hitting it. Direct sun creates harsh shadows and darkens parts of the product. The ideal light is an overcast sky or light filtered through a sheer curtain.

✅ RIGHT

Window with daylight, no direct sun. Product at 45 degree angle to the window. White or neutral background.

❌ WRONG

Direct sunlight on product. Phone flash turned on. Yellow ceiling lamp as the only light source.

Lighting Examples

Clothing and Fashion

Take advantage of broad natural light. Place the item near a glass door or large window. Diffused light shows the true colors of the fabric.

Jewelry and Accessories

Avoid natural sun which creates unwanted reflections. Use a front-facing ring light or a lightbox for total glare control.

Night Photography

Use two white LED bulbs (6500K) positioned on each side of the product. Never mix yellow and white light in the same shot.

Background: less is more

The background competes with the product. The messier it is, the more the customer's eye wanders away from what you are selling.

Seamless White Background

Use a large sheet of white paper or cardstock. Tape it to the wall and let it curve over the table. Ideal for almost all products.

Textures (Wood/Marble)

Buy patterned contact paper. It is very inexpensive and creates a sophisticated scene for cosmetics, perfumes, or food.

Dark Background

Use black fabric or dark wood. Works perfectly for premium items, gold tones, watches, or electronics.

How to hold the phone and frame the shot

Turn on the camera grid

In your phone settings, turn on the "Grid". With the grid active, place the product at the intersection of the lines instead of dead center. It looks much more attractive.

Choosing the best angle

Top-down (Flat Lay)

The camera is parallel to the table. Perfect for kits, stationery, open makeup palettes, or folded clothing.

Eye level (90°)

The camera faces the product at the exact same height. The best choice for bottles, shoes, mugs, and cosmetic jars.

45° Angle

The phone is slightly tilted pointing downwards. Shows the front and top of the product at once. Ideal for boxes and furniture.

Attention: Never use digital zoom on your phone. Zoom distorts the image and destroys quality. If you need a closer shot, physically move your phone closer to the product.

How many photos per product?

Marketplaces allow multiple photos. Fill all possible slots to clear up any doubts the customer might have.

  • Photo 1 (Hero): White background, full product focus. The cover photo.
  • Photo 2: Different angle, showing the side or back of the product.
  • Photo 3: Close-up detail shot (showing texture, label, or craftsmanship).
  • Photo 4: Lifestyle. The product being used by someone or set in a real environment.
  • Photo 5: Packaging photo so the customer knows exactly how they will receive the order.

Editing on your phone

You do not need a computer to edit. Use the phone's native editor or free apps like Lightroom Mobile.

  • Slightly increase exposure. Brighter, well-lit photos sell more.
  • Raise the "Shadows" level to illuminate very dark areas of the product.
  • Slightly reduce "Temperature/Warmth" if the photo turned out too yellow.
  • Slightly increase saturation to bring back the vibrancy of the colors.
Golden tip: Take three times as many photos as you think you need. You will end up discarding most of them, and this is a completely normal process even for professional photographers.
